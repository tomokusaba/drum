# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform
web

## Users
Assumption from the build brief: musicians and curious sound designers who want a quick, hands-on way to make and sequence synthesized drum sounds in a browser.

## Product Purpose
An accessible browser-based FM drum machine for finger drumming, shaping percussive sounds, and building short step-sequenced patterns.

## Positioning
The drum voices are generated by an in-house two-operator FM engine in the Web Audio API, not by a sample pack or a third-party synth library.

## Operating Context
Users play pads with touch, mouse, or keyboard, edit one drum voice at a time, and toggle steps in a repeating 16-step pattern. Audio starts only after an explicit user gesture.

## Capabilities and Constraints
- Initial release: eight drum-oriented voices, editable synthesis parameters, fixed-velocity pad triggering, four editable pattern slots, a 16-step sequencer, and tempo control.
- Live hits and sequenced hits use the same AudioWorklet synthesis engine and are scheduled against the audio clock.
- Multi-touch pad input and keyboard equivalents are required.
- The engine should remain separable and portable for a possible future WASM implementation; WASM is not required now.
- Persistence, recording/export, MIDI, variable velocity, and additional operators are undecided and out of scope for this MVP.

## Evidence on Hand
No existing product assets or user research were provided. Do not invent external endorsements or performance claims.

## Product Principles
- Make a sound quickly, with no audio before an intentional gesture.
- Keep the act of shaping a drum sound understandable and immediate.
- Make the sequence and its playback state easy to inspect.
- Keep every essential interaction available to keyboard and touch users.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
The interface targets WCAG 2.2 AA with semantic controls, programmatic labels, keyboard operability, visible focus, non-color-only states, touch targets aiming for at least 44 by 44 CSS pixels, and reduced-motion support.
